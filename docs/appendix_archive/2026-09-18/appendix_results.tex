\section{Numerical Confirmation of the Credit Identities}
\label{app:credit-suppression}

The identities of
\cref{thm:forward-backward,cor:near-mixing,thm:complete-mixing,cor:noise-threshold}
are closed-form claims about a Rule-projected gradient at a stated table
$\bm{P}_g$. None require training to check. This appendix confirms them
directly, at $K=16$ states and $M=52$ gates on the repository's
Boolean-circuit generator, by sampling perturbations
$\bm{P}_g=\bm{U}+\varepsilon(\bm{R}_g-\bm{U})$ for a random permutation $\bm{R}_g$ independent of
the true rule and computing the stated formulas in closed form. No model
is trained.

At $\bm{P}_g=\bm{U}$ ($D=6$, all $52$ gates, $12{,}000$ occurrences), the projected
process credit measures $15.0000=K-1$ at every occurrence, confirming
\cref{thm:complete-mixing}'s nonzero half numerically. The projected outcome
credit measures exactly $0$, as it must pointwise, not only in population
average: once any $\bm{P}_g=\bm{U}$ link is reached, every later forward signal
equals the uniform vector $\bm{u}$ exactly, and $\bm{\Pi}\bm{u}=0$ by definition of
$\bm{\Pi}$ --- this half is confirmed by construction rather than by the
measurement. It stays at floating-point zero across the noisy sweep
$\rho\in\{0,1/K,0.25,0.5,1\}$ as well, since $L_{\rm out}$ does not depend
on the corruption law. At $\varepsilon=0.4$, $D=8$, the forward and
backward residual norms individually vary over three orders of magnitude
across positions while their product, the conditional credit norm, is
constant to four significant figures, confirming \cref{thm:forward-backward}.
Across $\varepsilon\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4\}$ and
$D\in\{2,\ldots,12\}$, the measured credit norm stays at or below
\cref{cor:near-mixing}'s bound and a log-log fit recovers the predicted
exponent $D-1$ exactly ($R^2>0.999999$ for $D\ge5$). This holds in the
mixing-ball regime $\varepsilon\le0.5$ and should not be extrapolated
beyond it.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/credit_suppression.pdf}
 \caption{\textbf{Numerical confirmation of the credit identities.} Left:
 measured outcome credit norm against composition depth $D$ for each

 at its depth-independent value. Right: the fitted decay exponent at each

\Cref{cor:clean-convergence}'s clean ($\rho=1$) limit is confirmed the same
way: full-batch gradient descent from random logits on clean counts
reaches exact rule-cell recovery and $100\%$ greedy answer accuracy by
step $1{,}000$, while the mean predicted mass on the true successor climbs
toward, but by construction never reaches, one --- matching the
not-attained-at-finite-parameters part of that corollary.

\section{Earlier Experiments: Protocol and Results}
\label{app:legacy-protocol}

\subsection{Supervision comparison}
\label{app:supervision-comparison-protocol}
The comparison script trains the same learned GPT architecture separately on
outcome, process, corrupted, answer-first, and filler targets, resetting the
model seed per condition and sharing a minibatch seed and non-overlapping
train/test prompts.  Defaults: two blocks, four heads, embedding width $128$,
no dropout, AdamW at learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, zero weight decay, batch
size $128$, gradient clipping at one.  The script reports generated
final-answer accuracy, not complete-candidate local state margins, so these
metrics are not compared across the earlier experiments. \Cref{tab:supervision-comparison}
itself is a full rerun at these exact settings (seeds $2001$--$2005$, $8{,}000$
steps, $20{,}000$ train / $1{,}000$ held-out examples), launched as
\texttt{python -m src supervision --config
configs/experiments/e1\_five\_condition\_rerun.yaml}. The per-seed CSV,
full config, console log, and summary statistics are archived under
\texttt{results/paper/e1\_five\_condition\_rerun/}.

\paragraph{The clean-process discrepancy between the two runs.}
\Cref{sec:reliability} reports two clean-process aggregates for nominally
the same condition: $82.00_{\;\text{7.69}}\%$ from this five-condition
rerun and $92.6_{\;\text{2.9}}\%$ from the separately run reliability
sweep's clean endpoint (\cref{tab:legacy-circuit}), both at a nominally
identical architecture and update budget. A two-sample comparison
(Welch's $t$, unequal variances) puts this at $t\approx2.89$,
$\mathrm{df}\approx5.1$, $p\approx0.033$ across the two five-seed samples:
nominally significant despite the small samples, and not attributable to
chance alone at the conventional threshold. The two conditions' variances
also differ by a factor of about $7$ ($7.69\%$ against $2.9\%$ standard
deviation), so a handful of unusually low-answer-accuracy seeds in the
wider-variance condition materially drive the gap.

We traced this rather than leaving it unattributed. Two factors are
identified and quantified. First, git history places the reliability
sweep's archived run at a commit predating the current modular
\texttt{src/eval/reliability.py}, when the entry point was a since-replaced
top-level script defaulting to $100{,}000$ training instances rather than
this rerun's $20{,}000$. Retraining this five-condition rerun's clean
condition at $100{,}000$ instances (same $2001$--$2005$ seeds) raises the
mean to $85.31_{\;\text{8.17}}\%$. Second, the reliability sweep's
archived seeds are $\{2001,2002,2020,3000,2026\}$, not this rerun's
sequential $2001$--$2005$. Retraining at $100{,}000$ instances with that
exact seed set raises the mean further, to $88.41_{\;\text{4.11}}\%$.
Together these two factors close about $6.4$ of the $10.61$-point gap.
Third, we tested whether the remaining residual is instead a difference
between the two current code paths themselves --- \texttt{src.eval.supervision}
(used for this rerun) against \texttt{src.eval.reliability} (used for the
sweep) --- by running \texttt{src.eval.reliability}'s own $\rho=1$
condition at the identical $100{,}000$-instance, archived-seed
configuration just used for \texttt{src.eval.supervision}: it gives
$88.72_{\;\text{3.28}}\%$, statistically indistinguishable from
\texttt{src.eval.supervision}'s $88.41_{\;\text{4.11}}\%$ for the same
configuration. The two current pipelines therefore agree closely with
each other and are not the source of the remaining gap. Roughly $4$
percentage points to the archived $92.61\%$ stay unaccounted for, and we
do not extrapolate further from a comparison this underpowered. We
report all aggregates rather than picking one.

is assigned a clean trace when $U_i<\rho$, so the realized clean fraction

corrupt trace violates the execution rule while keeping the terminal answer
correct.  Saved fields distinguish final-answer accuracy, exact trace
accuracy, and average per-step accuracy, all free-running. The teacher-forced
state metrics used elsewhere condition on correct prefixes and are not the

\subsection{LoRA adaptation and local state scoring}
\label{app:lora-protocol}
The LoRA implementation adapts SmolLM2-135M on the depth-8 Boolean task with a
reliability sweep, an outcome-only baseline, and an optional answer-first
condition: rank-eight LoRA on all linear layers, $2{,}442{,}240$ trainable
parameters, $12{,}000$ training examples, $300$ held out, $800$ updates,
batch size $16$, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, adaptation seeds
$2001$--$2003$.  Reliability assignment here uses an exact-count permutation
rather than per-example Bernoulli draws, so the clean sets are nested with
exactly $\operatorname{round}(\rho N)$ clean examples.  For each held-out gold
prefix and each of the $16$ candidate states, the state margin is the gold
candidate's summed conditional log-probability minus the best competitor's;
the recorded local-correctness indicator is
strict positivity of this margin, and the all-local statistic averages
per-example products of this indicator across transitions rather than raising
mean local accuracy to the depth, evaluated against free generation rather
than assumed equal to it.  Fully reliable process supervision reaches
$89.67\%$ answer accuracy and $89.78\%$ exact rollout. At $\rho=0.50$, local
state accuracy is $82.49\%$ and exact rollout is $23.78\%$.  These
complete-state scores remain distinct from unconstrained tokenwise greedy
decoding. Raw per-seed generations, adapters, and local-margin CSVs for
every condition and seed are archived under
\texttt{results/paper/smollm/artifacts/}.

\section{Identifiability of the Executor from Corrupted Traces}
\label{app:noise-threshold}

This appendix gives the protocol and the full tables behind
\cref{sec:identifiability}.  It trains no
Transformer. The study measures finite-sample recovery under the same local
corruption law as \cref{sec:reliability}, using the shared transition-table model
of \cref{sec:operator-credit}.

 (a): the model's empirical optimum alongside the measured trained-model
 accuracy of \cref{tab:legacy-circuit}, against the threshold

 (b): the same corruption \emph{rate} with wrong successors drawn

 \label{fig:noise-threshold}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
\texttt{python experiments/run.py noise-threshold} imports the released Boolean gate
implementation and reproduces its conditional wrong-successor law. It uses
$K=16$, $M=52$ gate strings, depth $D=8$, $20{,}000$ circuits per corpus,
and three data seeds ($2001$--$2003$). The corpora are sampled separately from
the Transformer sweeps. Here, gates are uniform over the $52$ strings; the
sweeps first choose one of four gate families uniformly, then sample valid
indices within that family. Thus the studies share gate semantics and local
corruption, but differ in gate frequencies as well as model class and fitting
procedure. Each circuit receives a clean trace with probability $\rho$ or a
corrupted trace built recursively from its own displayed states otherwise.

\paragraph{Definitions.}
Let $n_{gij}$ count displayed transitions from $i$ to $j$ under gate $g$.
The \emph{empirical optimum} is the row-normalized table
$\widehat Q_{gij}=n_{gij}/\sum_b n_{gib}$ for every observed row. It minimizes
the empirical process cross-entropy over row-stochastic tables. The
\emph{population optimum} is instead the analytic conditional law
$\bm{Q}_g=\rho \bm{T}_g+(1-\rho)\bm{C}_g$. The separate gradient-descent fit takes $4{,}000$
full-batch steps with learning rate $5$ from $\mathcal N(0,1)$ logits on the
empirical loss. \emph{Rule cells} is the fraction of the $KM=832$
$(g,i)$ pairs whose most probable successor is correct. \emph{Answer accuracy}
executes these decoded rules on $1{,}000$ held-out circuits, also sampled
uniformly over gate strings. Both fits use displayed local pairs only, and neither
uses the separately supplied correct final answer as an additional target.

\paragraph{The gradient direction at complete mixing.}
Projecting the empirical process gradient at $\bm{P}_g=\bm{U}$ onto $\bm{T}_g-\bm{U}$ and averaging
over gates reproduces \cref{eq:symmetric-credit-proof}: the coefficient is
$-0.0667$ at $\rho=0$ against the predicted $-0.0667$, $+0.0458$ at $\rho=0.10$
against $+0.0400$, $+0.4659$ at $\rho=0.50$ against $+0.4667$, and $+1.0000$ at
$\rho=1$; the mean cosine with $\bm{T}_g-\bm{U}$ crosses zero between $\rho=0.03$ and
$\rho=0.0625$.  The deviations from the analytic coefficients reflect finite sampling. The
population coefficient is zero specifically at $\rho=1/K$.

\paragraph{Recovery, and what gradient descent reaches.}
\Cref{tab:noise-recovery} gives the empirical optimum alongside the measured
trained-model accuracies.  Gradient descent from random logits on the same
corpora, using only displayed local pairs, recovers $58.89\%$ of rule cells
and $8.60\%$ answer accuracy at $\rho=0.10$, $99.40\%$ and $95.70\%$ at
$\rho=0.20$, and $100\%$ and $100\%$ at $\rho=0.50$. Refitting under
family-first gate sampling instead of the uniform-over-strings sampling
used above moves the $50\%$-answer-accuracy crossing only from
$\rho\approx0.127$ to $\rho\approx0.138$
(\texttt{python experiments/run.py family-sampling}) --- both far from the
Transformer sweep's $\rho\approx0.85$, so gate sampling is not what
separates the two frontiers.  The finite-sample
frontier sits near $\rho=0.15$ rather than at the population threshold
$1/K=6.25\%$, which is the plurality-versus-population effect described in
\cref{app:noise-threshold-proof}.

\paragraph{The fully corrupted corpus.}
At $\rho=0$ the true successor is absent from all $832$ cells
($100.00\%$ of cells have zero count on it).  Decoding each row by its least
frequent successor recovers $100.00\%$ of rule cells and gives $100.0\%$ greedy
answer accuracy; decoding by the most frequent successor, which is the row
maximizer of the cross-entropy optimum, recovers $0.00\%$ and gives $6.8\%$.
For context, the separate trained-model aggregate under fully corrupted traces
is $6.12_{\;\text{1.06}}\%$ (\cref{tab:supervision-comparison}).

\paragraph{Structure against rate.}
\Cref{tab:noise-structure} replaces the uniform wrong successor by a fixed
relabelling $\sigma$ of the valid one, holding the corruption rate fixed; this
is an incorrect permutation with no row agreeing with the true rule.
\Cref{prop:noisy-process-credit} predicts $\rho_c=1/2$ for this alternative.
The measurements are consistent with the predicted dependence on the
conditional corruption law, with finite-sample deviations near each
threshold, and the dissociation between the two thresholds holds under
family-first gate sampling as well (\texttt{python experiments/run.py
tabular-sampling}), so it is not an artifact of which gate distribution the
tabular corpora use.

\paragraph{The same statement in counts.}
Holding the number of valid traces fixed at $N_+=2{,}000$ and adding corrupted
ones, \cref{eq:symmetric-credit-proof} predicts that the population direction
is positive for $N_-<(K-1)N_+=30{,}000$, zero at equality, and negative above it.  Measured rule cells and answer accuracy
are $100.00\%/100.0\%$ at $N_-=2{,}000$, $94.59\%/65.1\%$ at $N_-=10{,}000$,
$46.15\%/7.2\%$ at $N_-=20{,}000$, $8.17\%/6.2\%$ at the predicted crossing
$N_-=30{,}000$, and $0.00\%/5.6\%$ at $N_-=60{,}000$.  The finite-sample
collapse again precedes the population crossing, for the reason given in
\cref{app:noise-threshold-proof}; the direction of the effect and the location
of the population crossing are what the calculation predicts.

\subsection{Fraction versus amount}
\label{app:fraction-vs-amount}
The sweeps above vary $\rho$ at one fixed corpus size. They do not by
themselves show that the gap between the population threshold and a
finite-sample measurement closes with more \emph{data} at a fixed
reliability, rather than requiring a higher $\rho$. This experiment
(\texttt{python experiments/run.py fraction-vs-amount}) holds $\rho$ fixed
at several values above $1/K=6.25\%$ and varies the corpus size $N$ alone,
using the same corrupted-count corpora, decoding, and scoring as above,
three data seeds. At $\rho=0.10$, rule cells recovered rise from
$12.26\%$ at $N=500$ to $90.14\%$ at $N=50{,}000$, against a $N=400{,}000$
population ceiling of $100.00\%$; answer accuracy over the same range rises
from $6.03\%$ to $49.00\%$, against a ceiling of $100.00\%$. At $\rho=0.30$,
comfortably above threshold, both measures already saturate by
$N=5{,}000$--$10{,}000$ ($99.36\%$ rule cells, $95.30\%$ answer accuracy).
At $\rho=0.08$, just above $1/K$, convergence is slowest: rule cells reach
only $45.07\%$ and answer accuracy $6.90\%$ by $N=50{,}000$, against a
ceiling of $99.28\%/94.50\%$ at $N=400{,}000$ --- the reliability is high
enough to recover in principle, but the margin over chance is thin enough
that a large corpus is needed. Holding $\rho$ fixed and increasing $N$ moves
every measured curve toward its own population ceiling without $\rho$ ever
changing, isolating the amount effect from the fraction effect
(\cref{fig:fraction-vs-amount}).

\section{Gradient-Level Credit Asymmetry in the Trained Transformer}
\label{app:gradient-alignment}

This appendix gives the full protocol, per-seed results, and per-transition
breakdown behind \cref{sec:gradient-alignment}'s diagnostic. It trains the
same two-block GPT, architecture, and optimizer already used for
\cref{tab:supervision-comparison} (\cref{app:legacy-protocol}). No new
architecture, task, or benchmark is introduced.

\paragraph{Protocol.} \texttt{python -m src.experiments.gradient\_alignment}
trains one process-mode model per seed ($2001$--$2003$) on $20{,}000$
depth-8 Boolean-circuit instances for $8{,}000$ steps (batch $128$,
embedding $128$, $4$ heads, $2$ layers, AdamW at $3\times10^{-4}$ ---
identical to \cref{app:legacy-protocol}'s supervision comparison), with a
$256$-instance held-out reference batch (seed $101$, disjoint from
training) fixed across all checkpoints. No model checkpoint is written to
disk: at each of the four checkpoints (step $0$, $2{,}000$, $4{,}000$,
$8{,}000$), the live model is evaluated in place. For each of the
reference instances, three teacher-forced cross-entropy losses are built
from the same underlying instance (prompt and gold answer), but --- this
matters, so we are explicit --- \emph{not} from a common token sequence:
the process loss and the oracle local-transition loss share one encoded
sequence, prompt followed by the displayed trace and the answer, and
differ only in which character positions are masked into the loss (every
trace and answer character for the process loss; only the four state-bit
characters after each gate's \texttt{>} for the oracle loss, excluding gate
specifiers, separators, and the answer). The counterfactual outcome loss
instead encodes a wholly separate, trace-free sequence --- prompt followed
directly by the answer, with no trace tokens anywhere in the input or the
target --- identical in construction to \cref{tab:supervision-comparison}'s
own \textsc{Outcome} condition, and evaluated at the process-trained
parameters rather than parameters $L_{\rm out}$ itself ever optimized.
State-bit positions for the oracle loss are identified directly from the
generator's own trace string (\texttt{gate>state gate>state \ldots}),
exploiting the character-level tokenizer's one-to-one offset
correspondence. Each loss is backpropagated separately (zeroing gradients
between the three), and the resulting flattened gradients are compared by
dot product, norm, and cosine similarity. The per-transition breakdown
repeats this with the oracle loss restricted to one gate's four state bits
at a time. The canonical per-seed CSV underlying
\cref{tab:gradient-alignment,tab:gradient-alignment-perseed,tab:gradient-alignment-positions}
is in \texttt{results/gradient\_alignment\_archived\_3seed/}, including all
$t=0,\ldots,7$ breakdowns. Its exact path, SHA-256, and reproduction
command are identified in \texttt{results/paper/ARTIFACT\_MANIFEST.md}.
Later reruns are listed separately and do not supply these tables.

\section{A Concrete Instance of Group-Symmetry Non-Identifiability}
\label{app:shift-symmetry-task}

\Cref{thm:group-symmetry-nonidentifiability} needs a gate family whose
generated permutation group has a nontrivial centralizer element of order
dividing $D$; \cref{app:identifiability-certificate} below shows this
paper's actual Boolean-circuit gate family does not admit one (in the
stronger, correct sense of \cref{thm:fixed-depth-identifiability}). This
appendix exhibits a closely related family, restricted from the same
Boolean gates, where a nontrivial element provably exists, to confirm the
theorem's hypothesis is satisfiable and not vacuous, rather than as a
trained-network experiment: this is a worked example of the construction,
not an empirical validation of it.

\paragraph{Construction.} Fix one state coordinate as a \emph{sink bit}
(bit $4$ of the paper's $4$-bit Boolean states) and restrict the gate
alphabet to gates that do not read the sink bit or permute it into another
coordinate: it may only be flipped, conditionally or unconditionally, by a
gate whose target is the sink bit and whose controls (if any) are among
the other three bits. Concretely, from this paper's $52$ Boolean gate
strings (\cref{sec:background}), this excludes every $\texttt{c}$/$\texttt{t}$
string with the sink bit as a control and every $\texttt{s}$(SWAP) string
touching the sink bit --- SWAP moves the sink bit's value into another
coordinate even though it has no separate ``control,'' so ``sink bit only
as a target'' is not quite the right restriction by itself. Enumerating
the released generator directly leaves $31$ of the $52$ strings ($4$
\texttt{x}, $9$ \texttt{c}, $6$ \texttt{s}, $12$ \texttt{t}), verified by
direct computation to be exactly the gates commuting with
$\bm A:=$ ``flip the sink bit,'' an involution ($\bm A^2=\bm I$). Since
$D=8$ is even, \cref{thm:group-symmetry-nonidentifiability} applies:
$\bm T'_g:=\bm T_g\bm A$ differs from $\bm T_g$ at every one of the $16$
states, for every one of the $31$ gates (verified by direct computation),
yet $\bm T'_{g_1}\cdots\bm T'_{g_8}=\bm T_{g_1}\cdots\bm T_{g_8}$ for every
8-gate word from this restricted alphabet (verified over $20{,}000$ random
words), while the same check at the odd depth $D=7$ disagrees on every
word tested, confirming the parity dependence on $\bm A^D=\bm I$. Unlike
an abelian toy example, this alphabet is non-abelian --- a NOT on bit $1$
and a CNOT controlled by bit $1$ do not commute with each other --- so the
ambiguity is not merely a relabeling of a degenerate, commutative
computation. The restricted task (\texttt{shift\_symmetry\_8}, defined in
\texttt{src/data/sequential\_tasks.py}'s
\texttt{make\_shift\_symmetry\_sampler}\footnote{The released sampler uses
the $\mathbb Z_{16}$ additive-shift construction from an earlier revision
of this appendix, which the same theorem also covers (an abelian group
trivially has the required nontrivial central element) but which is a more
degenerate instance than the sink-bit family described here; both satisfy
the theorem's hypothesis.}) is released with the rest of the codebase; we
do not report a trained-network measurement on it, since a hidden-state
linear probe would test whether a particular residual-stream position
happens to linearly encode $s_t$ or $s'_t$, not the theorem's actual claim,
which is about what terminal observations determine, independent of any
particular network's internal representation.

\subsection{A Computational Identifiability Certificate for the Boolean Task}
\label{app:identifiability-certificate}

\Cref{thm:fixed-depth-identifiability} makes the Boolean-circuit family's
identifiability a checkable computation rather than an assumption: form
$H=\langle\bm T_g\bm T_h^{-1}:g,h\rangle\le S_{16}$ from all $52$ released
gate permutations and compute its centralizer in $S_{16}$. Reproduced by
\texttt{python -m src.experiments.identifiability\_certificate}, which
imports the released gate implementation directly rather than
re-deriving it: $H$ has order $16!/2=10{,}461{,}394{,}944{,}000$ and every
generator is an even permutation, so $H=A_{16}$ exactly (not merely a
subgroup of matching order); \texttt{sympy.combinatorics} computes
$C_{S_{16}}(H)=\{\bm I\}$. By \cref{thm:fixed-depth-identifiability}, the
unique zero-loss row-stochastic executor for this task, at any depth and
under full-support training, is $\bm P_g=\bm T_g$: this rules out an
alternative exact factorization of the terminal map as the explanation for
the measured depth-$8$ outcome failure (\cref{tab:depth-capacity}), leaving
the credit-based account of \cref{sec:operator-credit} as the explanation
this paper analyzes.
