
\paragraph{The random seed.}\label{sec:seed}
All masks are full causal masks. The initialization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:init}
\theta_0=\varepsilon G+\delta A .
\end{equation}
In $G$, every weight and embedding block other than the readout has i.i.d.\ $\mathcal N(0,1/m)$ entries, with $m$ the larger of its two dimensions; the readout block of $G$ is zero, its relative biases are zero, and its scaled MLP biases are fixed signs, $G_{b}\in\{\pm1\}^{w}$. The perturbation $A$ is deterministic and task-aligned (for example, weak positional or match-sensitive directions), vanishes on the readout and the biases, and satisfies $\delta\|A_\mathrm B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\varepsilon/4$ on every block $\mathrm B$. Because the readout is zero, every initial next-token distribution is uniform: the seed does not count. Every tensor of \cref{eq:block}, the relative biases included, is trained by the same Euclidean flow $\dot\theta=-\nabla_\theta\mathcal L$, with $\mathcal L=\Lout$ or $\Lproc$. Nothing is frozen, tied, projected or rescaled.

\begin{lemma}[The random seed is a small initialization]\label{lem:seed}
Let $N$ be the number of parameter blocks and $m_{\min}$ the smallest larger-dimension among them, and suppose the residual width is at least $16$ times the vocabulary size. With probability at least $1-2(N+1)e^{-m_{\min}/32}$ over $G$, the seed \cref{eq:init} satisfies \cref{ass:init} with $a_0=4$, $c_{\mathrm{emb}}=1/4$, $r_0=1/16$ and $N_b=\sqrt w$, and has zero readout.
\end{lemma}
The proof (\cref{app:seed}) combines the Gaussian singular-value bounds of \citet[Chapter~7]{vershynin2018high} with Weyl's inequality. At executor widths, $m_{\min}=\Theta(n)$, so the failure probability is $e^{-\Omega(n)}$.

\section{Exact executors and main results}\label{sec:results}
\begin{proposition}[Exact Outcome and Step Executors Exist in the Same Two-Block Transformer]\label{prop:represent}
For every $n\ge2$, $K\ge2$ and finite $\Sigma$, the architecture of \cref{eq:block} with full causal masks, three heads per block, $d_{\mathrm h}=2n+6$, $d_{\mathrm{res}}=2|\Sigma|+4K+d_{\mathrm h}+3$, and MLP width $\max\{|\Sigma|,3K(n+1),2n+1\}$ admits separate parameter settings that greedily generate the entire running-count continuation and the direct final-count continuation on every prompt. Every required prediction has strictly positive logit margin.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Construction idea]
Block 1 retrieves the query and computes its match with each letter by a ReLU equality test. The step executor retrieves the previous state and the next match bit and applies a modular lookup. The outcome executor averages match bits, rescales to the count, retrieves $s_0$, and applies a triangular-ReLU lookup. Finite softmax scores make retrieval approximate, but the explicit leakage bound preserves the logit margins. The complete construction and finite-score proof are in \cref{app:architectures}; exhaustive verification is separate in \cref{app:verify}.
\end{proof}
The executors are far from the small seed; the proposition says only that both programs exist in the trained parameter space.

\begin{theorem}[Outcome plateau from a random seed]\label{thm:outcome}\label{thm:outcome-end}
Fix $K\ge2$, $p\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}$ and $n\ge2$. There exist $c_{\mathrm{init}},c_\lambda,C_2>0$, depending only on $K$ and the block/head counts, such that the following holds whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small}
0<\varepsilon\le\min\{1/2,\ c_{\mathrm{init}}\min(1,w^{-1/2}),\ c_\lambda\lambda\}.
\end{equation}
On the event of \cref{lem:seed}, let $\theta(t)$ follow the gradient flow of $\Lout$ on every parameter from \cref{eq:init}, and let $r_0=1/16$. For $0\le t\le T_{\mathrm{out}}:=r_0^2\lambda^{-n}/(2C_2)$,
\begin{align}
\|\theta(t)-\theta_0\|&\le r_0\varepsilon,\nonumber\\
\log K-C_2\varepsilon^2\lambda^n\le\Lout(\theta(t))&\le\log K,\label{eq:plateau}\\
\left|\E[\softmax(z_{\mathrm{ans}}(\theta(t)))_y]-1/K\right|&\le\sqrt{C_2/2}\,\varepsilon\lambda^{n/2}.\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
The constants are the local-bound constants of \cref{lem:score-sum,lem:bias-uniform}, not measured values. The proof is in \cref{app:outcome}; \cref{lem:seed} supplies its initialization hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional polynomial-time process learning]\label{thm:process}
Let $\theta(t)$ follow the gradient flow of $\Lproc$ on every parameter from \cref{eq:init}, and fix $\xi\in(0,1]$ and $\tau=\xi\log2/M$. Assume, on an event of probability at least $1-\zeta$ over the seed:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(P\arabic*),ref=(P\arabic*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item\label{hyp:regular} \emph{Regularity.} An absolutely continuous solution exists, with $\frac{d}{dt}\Lproc(\theta(t))=-\|\nabla_\theta\Lproc(\theta(t))\|^2$ almost everywhere. (At zero readout, $\Lproc(\theta_0)=L_0:=\log|\mathcal V|$, with $\mathcal V$ the vocabulary.)
\item\label{hyp:align} \emph{Persistent alignment.} There are $B_n,a_n>0$ and, at every time with $\Lproc(\theta(t))>\tau$, a direction $D(t)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:align}
\|D(t)\|\le B_n,\qquad -\bigl\langle\nabla_\theta\Lproc(\theta(t)),D(t)\bigr\rangle\ge a_n\,\Lproc(\theta(t)).
\end{equation}
\item\label{hyp:poly} \emph{Polynomial constants.} $B_n,\ a_n^{-1}\le\mathrm{poly}(n)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\Lproc(\theta(t))\le\tau$ for all
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proc-time}
t\ \ge\ T_{\mathrm{proc}}:=\frac{M B_n^2}{a_n^2\,\xi\log2},
\end{equation}
and at every such time greedy decoding from a fresh prompt produces the entire gold continuation $s_1,\ldots,s_n,{:},s_n,\mathrm{EOS}$ with probability at least $1-\xi$. If $\xi^{-1}\le\mathrm{poly}(n)$, then $T_{\mathrm{proc}}=\mathrm{poly}(n)$.
\end{theorem}
The direction $D(t)$ is only a witness: training follows the actual gradient of every tensor. The proof (\cref{app:process}) integrates $\dot L\le-(a_n/B_n)^2L^2$ and bounds a free-running error by its first error, where the generated prefix still equals the gold one. No assumption about uniform transfer across histories is needed.

\begin{proposition}[Readout separability implies alignment]\label{prop:separable}
Write the logits at a supervised context $x$ as $z_\theta(x)=V h_\Phi(x)$, where $V$ is the readout, $h_\Phi(x)$ the final residual vector and $\Phi$ all other parameters. Suppose that until $\Lproc\le\tau$, for almost every supervised context $x$ with gold token $y=y(x)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(B\arabic*),ref=(B\arabic*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item\label{hyp:sep} there is $U(t)$ with $\|U(t)\|_F\le B_n$ and $(U_y-U_k)^\top h_{\Phi(t)}(x)\ge\gamma_n$ for every token $k\ne y$;
\item\label{hyp:lossbd} the per-context loss obeys $\ell_x(\theta(t))\le H_n$.
\end{enumerate}
Then \ref{hyp:align} holds with $D(t)=(U(t)\text{ on }V,\ 0\text{ elsewhere})$ and $a_n=\gamma_n/(1+H_n)$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep-time}
T_{\mathrm{proc}}\le\frac{M B_n^2(1+H_n)^2}{\gamma_n^2\,\xi\log2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\ref{hyp:sep} concerns the real, evolving features; only the witness $U$ lives in readout coordinates. It is measurable along an unrestricted run (\cref{app:diagnostics}), but a finite sample cannot establish it for the whole population.

\begin{corollary}[Matched separation, conditional on alignment]\label{cor:separation}
Let both flows start from the same seed \cref{eq:init} with $\varepsilon=c_*n^{-1/2}$, widths $\Theta(n)$ and fixed $K,p$, and suppose \ref{hyp:regular}--\ref{hyp:poly} hold for the process flow on the event of \cref{lem:seed}, with $\xi^{-1}\le\mathrm{poly}(n)$. For $c_*$ small and all sufficiently large $n$, $T_{\mathrm{proc}}<T_{\mathrm{out}}$, and on $[T_{\mathrm{proc}},T_{\mathrm{out}}]$ the process model generates the whole continuation on all but a $\xi$ fraction of prompts while the outcome model satisfies \cref{eq:plateau}.
\end{corollary}
Both flows use every parameter and the same Euclidean metric, so no metric comparison is needed. At a finite horizon the exact inequality $T_{\mathrm{proc}}<T_{\mathrm{out}}$ must be checked; quoting orders does not establish it.

\paragraph{What the observables mean.}
The outcome bound controls the average probability assigned to the correct answer; it does not bound greedy accuracy, which can be sensitive to arbitrarily small logit differences. The process conclusion is a probability over fresh prompts, not a guarantee on every prompt. Neither result identifies which internal heads or MLP units carry the computation.
