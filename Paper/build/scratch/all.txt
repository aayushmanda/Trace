A Transformer can represent a counting algorithm yet struggle to learn it from
ﬁnal answers. We explain how running-count supervision changes the gradient
signal. Each unseen letter contracts a feature’s correlation with the ﬁnal count,
while small initialization favors features that read few letters. In a two-block
Transformer containing exact solutions for both formats, outcome cross-entropy
gradient ﬂow from a zero readout therefore stays near chance for exponential time.
Displayed states expose a local innovation: rule credit is generically nonzero
and has a quantitative Gaussian lower bound under a quantitative geometric initialization event. Structured joint gradient ﬂow learns transitions in polynomial
time, suppresses clean distractors, and, in a separate tied feature model, learns
the current-letter relative offset. The positive results retain speciﬁed feature roles
and training metrics. Experiments support the mechanism beyond these assumptions, including LoRA-tuned object tracking, but a ﬁve-object reversal shows its
empirical limits.

1 Introduction
If one Transformer can represent both a ﬁnal-answer algorithm and a step-by-step algorithm, why
can it learn one much more easily? We compare supervision formats for one ﬁxed modular-counting
target, holding the architecture and experimental training budget ﬁxed. Intermediate supervision is
known to help reasoning [Nye et al., 2021, Wei et al., 2022, Lightman et al., 2023]; counting lets us
trace that beneﬁt through the credit each network feature receives.

27

A feature that misses letters loses a factor λ < 1 in ﬁnal-count correlation per unseen letter. Small
initialization makes such local features dominate the network’s gradient. Displaying the previous
state instead exposes one update and identiﬁes a useful state–letter interaction. The data descriptor
λ connects hidden-step contraction to local innovation. For binary transition learning, the sum of
the two correct-class margins measures the learned letter-dependent rule; their minimum determines
success (Equation (5)). Our theory connects the availability of credit to the growth of this rule in
three controlled parameterizations.

Scope of Theorem 4. The theorem guarantees optimization within structured feature families. The
embeddings and value directions represent the displayed state and current letter, but the transition
readouts and the query–key interaction start untrained and evolve together. Part (i) covers aligned
and nondegenerate misaligned scalar initializations with supplied routing; part (ii) learns suppression of clean distractors and proves persistent margins, not Lstep → 0. The full-preﬁx result (Theorem D.1) relaxes routing in a different tied-feature family and does not follow from part (i). Fixed
off-direction perturbations of the planted query–key directions are covered by part (iii) for K = 2
and aligned starts; trained off-family parameters and value perturbations are not (Appendix D.6).

Finding 1: outcome learning tracks accessible low-degree signal. In the controlled smallinitialization regime, exit times from the theorem’s ball grow exponentially with horizon while step
exit times stay 0.10–0.16; reﬁned full-batch updates verify this at ﬁxed ReLU signs (Table 6). The
fast step exit ﬁts the state-only prior, so it is distinct from rule learning. At n = 16, paired AdamW
runs reach 93.1% step accuracy (8/10 solved seeds) and 49.8% outcome accuracy. Across the wider
horizon/contraction robustness grid, outcome accuracy correlates 0.91 with λn ; after subtracting the
start-state prior, the correlation is 0.15 and the median excess is 0.2 points (Figure 3). Jacobian
energy stays overwhelmingly at degrees 0 and 1 until escape. Modulo-3 at n = 8 is an exception:
two of three outcome seeds solve within 32k updates (Appendix E.3).
Finding 2: ﬁnite-step training can learn routing. Training QK scores, relative biases and readout jointly from a fan-in random initialization at ε = 0.3 solves 16 of 17 step runs; the remaining
run reaches 99.95%. Matched outcome runs solve none within 300k updates. Median solve time
grows from 44k at n = 6 to 237k at n = 16, rather than the 1,024-fold growth of λ−n (Table 2).
Values, embeddings and MLPs remain ﬁxed in these runs. They leave the theorem’s structured family in three ways: the query–key maps are fan-in random matrices, training uses ﬁnite steps, and the
loss supervises every running count, including the t = 1 boundary case. They are evidence beyond
Theorem 4, not an instance of it.
Finding 3: the planted gate tolerates query–key perturbations far beyond the proved radius,
not value perturbations. Theorem 4(iii) certiﬁes ﬁxed query–key perturbations of relative size 18 .

Our comparison ﬁxes the target and architecture, then traces the gradient credit that each format
supplies.
Weak target-dependent gradients appear in composed-target and parity analyses [Shalev-Shwartz
et al., 2017, Abbe et al., 2021, 2022, 2023, 2025]. Our outcome result instead concerns Transformer
cross-entropy ﬂow, with time set by count contraction. Low-degree signal from intermediate checkpoints [Panigrahi et al., 2025], Efron–Stein analysis [Li and Mossel, 2025], sensitivity bias [Hahn
and Roﬁn, 2024], and spectral simplicity bias [Yang and Salman, 2019] offer related views. We use
degree spectra as measurements; the proofs condition directly on the letters a network term reads.

255

Kim and Suzuki [2025] prove teacher-forced parity learning with position-only attention and a ﬁxed
nonlinearity. Our transition proofs train the interaction, and the full-preﬁx result learns a relative
offset in a tied feature family. Wang et al. [2025] give statistical-query and curriculum results for
hidden permutations. Modular counting is a cyclic automaton, whose shallow shortcuts can be
constructed [Liu et al., 2023]; expressivity alone does not establish that training ﬁnds them.

The negative results apply near small initialization with a ﬁxed ReLU pattern; the outcome plateau
uses population gradient ﬂow. The positive results retain structured feature roles, and the learnedoffset theorem additionally uses tied factors and target-dependent ﬁxed mobilities. Beyond random
scalar magnitudes and independent distractor biases, robustness of the planted seed is proved for
ﬁxed query–key perturbations of relative size 18 at K = 2 with aligned starts. Larger and trained
perturbations, K ≥ 3 and misaligned starts are measured, not proved, and value perturbations can
redirect learning (Table 4). The Gaussian lower bound concerns initial rule credit, not the full loss
gradient. The matched outcome separation is established for the supplied-routing seed; it does not
transfer automatically to the count-dependent ReLU seed of the full-preﬁx result.

274

Joint discovery of routing and unrestricted features from random weights remains open. With the
value features ﬁxed, it reduces to one step: once every state has a letter-contrast ﬂoor, the readouts
provably ﬁnish in polynomial time while the query–key maps keep training (Lemma D.19). Gaussian query–key maps start with the useful gate acceleration in every state (Proposition D.2), and in
all 240 measured runs they form and keep the ﬂoor. Proving that they do is the open step. Finitestep routing experiments and pretrained-model transfer extend beyond the proof regime, and the
ﬁve-object reversal prevents treating low-degree signal as a complete optimization predictor. The
controlled counting task explains one mechanism by which displayed states make learning easier; it
does not yet characterize when that mechanism governs broader reasoning.

Whole head and control. When v, ∆V and the readout also train, every run is certiﬁed, 15–55 times
faster than the exact seed, because the value scale is no longer ﬁxed at ε. The share using the planted

the planted directions are therefore not what makes the task learnable here. They are what makes
the reduction of Lemma D.3 exact.
Random query–key maps with ﬁxed values. Lemma D.19 proves completion once a statewise
gate ﬂoor exists, for any query–key dynamics. To test whether unplanted query–key maps create
that ﬂoor, we drop the planted part entirely: WQ , WK are Gaussian with ρ ∈ {0.25, 1, 4} times
the planted Frobenius norms and train as full matrices, WV = vΠV stays ﬁxed, and the readouts
are those of the theorem. Over K ∈ {2, 3}, ε ∈ {0.2, 0.3} and 20 seeds per cell, all 240 runs are
certiﬁed through a gate. In every run, once the weakest oriented contrast mins (r0s − r1s ) reaches 0.05
it never falls below 0.05 again, and the time from that point to certiﬁcation satisﬁes Lemma D.19,
using at most 0.03% of its bound. At ε = 0.3 these runs take 1.0–2.2 times the planted seed’s time.
With the value features ﬁxed, the gate ﬂoor is therefore observed to form and persist from random
query–key maps. Proving that it does is the open step.
